n systems, m attackers: how the penetration score behaves as n grows

Statistics 2026 · Homework on the Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem. Everything on this page is computed in your browser with JavaScript; no libraries.

Experiment

Each attacker visits n = 1 … 10 000 systems one after the other. The values for n = 10, 100, 1000, … are read from the same trajectories.

1 · The model

There are n systems and m attackers. Attacker j goes through the systems one by one and gets a score X1, X2, … Xn. All the scores are independent and identically distributed, so the m attackers are m independent copies of the same random trajectory.

  • Bernoulli: Xi = 1 if the system is penetrated, 0 otherwise, P(Xi = 1) = p. Then μ = p and σ² = p(1−p).
  • Rademacher: Xi = +1 or −1 with probability ½ each (a fair fight). Then μ = 0 and σ² = 1.

For the three quantities of the assignment, independence gives E[Sn] = nμ and Var(Sn) = nσ², and dividing by a constant c divides the variance by c²:

Sn totalnμnσ²spread grows like √n
Sn/n averageμσ²/nspread shrinks, Sn/n → μ in probability (LLN)
Sn/√n normalized√n·μσ²spread constant; Rademacher: Sn/√n → N(0,1) in distribution (CLT)

Only the √n normalization keeps the variance fixed. Without division it explodes; dividing by n makes the distribution collapse onto a point, so there is no shape left to look at.

5 · Law of Large Numbers: Sn/n → μ in probability

Convergence in probability means: for every ε > 0, P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) → 0. The chart estimates this probability as the fraction of the m attackers whose average score is farther than ε from μ, for three values of ε.

A smaller ε needs a larger n before the curve reaches zero, but it reaches zero for every ε. This is Chebyshev's inequality at work: P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²) → 0.

6 · Central Limit Theorem

Let Zn = (Sn − nμ)/(σ√n). In the Rademacher model μ = 0 and σ = 1, so Zn = Sn/√n exactly, and the CLT says Zn → N(0,1). In the Bernoulli model the raw Sn/√n has mean p√n, which runs away, so it must be centred first (this is why the black curve in the third histogram row moves).

The chart measures the distance from the normal with the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic D = maxz |F̂m(z) − Φ(z)|, the largest vertical gap between the empirical CDF of the m attackers and the standard normal CDF Φ.

D decreases as n grows, until it reaches the noise level of the experiment: even a sample of size m taken from a perfect normal has D ≈ 0.87/√m, so below that line the remaining gap cannot be told apart from sampling noise. A larger m lowers this floor.

7 · Interpretation: n systems and m attackers

Effect of the number of systems n

  • Bernoulli. The total grows like np, but its spread grows only like √(np(1−p)), so the relative uncertainty shrinks like 1/√n. The success rate Sn/n of each attacker becomes almost the same number, p. On a small network two equally skilled attackers can look very different; on a large one their success rates are almost equal. For a defender this means the fraction of the network that falls is predictable, and it only decreases if p decreases.
  • Rademacher. Gains and losses cancel on average, so the average score goes to 0, but a single attacker's net score is typically of size √n: some end well ahead, some well behind, with no difference in skill. Dividing by √n keeps this spread visible and gives the bell shape.

Effect of the number of attackers m

m does not change the score distribution of any single attacker. It changes how well the simulation shows that distribution (histograms get smoother, the LLN and KS curves get less noisy) and it changes the aggregate score T = Sn(1) + … + Sn(m), which is a sum of nm independent increments: E[T] = nmμ and Var(T) = nmσ². So T/(nm) obeys the LLN with nm in place of n, and adding attackers has the same effect on the aggregate as adding systems. The table uses the first m′ attackers of the current simulation.

Limits of the model

Everything above needs independent and identically distributed scores. Real systems are not equally hard, attackers learn from one system to the next and one stolen password can open many systems at once. With such dependence the variance of Sn can grow faster than n and the √n scaling would not describe the fluctuations any more.
